Recorded implementation notes:
- Rooms and unjoined security codes expire after 10 minutes.
- After joining, a room ends when a participant disconnects or the connection is lost.
- Use localhost for development and environment variables for API/WebSocket URLs and CORS. Do not hard-code a LAN IP.
- Use a six-digit cryptographically random temporary room code; it is not an encryption key.
- Use anonymous You / Partner labels by default, with optional temporary session-only display names and no permanent accounts.
- Use a cybersecurity-themed dark navy/black and cyan/blue interface. Do not use official MIT/MAHE branding without approved assets; keep branding free.
- Use X25519 + HKDF, and show SHA-256 public-key fingerprints for manual verification. Do not present X25519 by itself as identity authentication; avoid paid certificate or identity infrastructure.
- Use synthetic/public-domain images around 100 KB, 500 KB, 1 MB, 5 MB, and 10 MB. Benchmark text sizes of 1 KB, 10 KB, 100 KB, 500 KB, and 1 MB.

Security note: the Phase 5-13 prototype encrypts chat text and image chunks in the browsers with AES-256-GCM. The key exchange is not authenticated automatically; partners should compare fingerprints through a separate trusted channel. Image transfer is limited to 10 MB. The chat displays a SQLite-backed hash-chain event log and checks its integrity; this detects record edits but is not a signed or externally anchored proof. API request throttling and replay tracking use in-memory state and reset when the backend restarts. Traffic analysis, benchmarks, and broader security testing remain future work.
